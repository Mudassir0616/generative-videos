## Motion language
- **The caption is the animation.** Each word is its own element. It appears **within about ±0.2 s of being spoken** (typically 0–0.2 s early; on headline lines the whole phrase can get up to 0.9 s ahead, e.g. "MOTION SKILL"). Its entrance:
  - opacity 0 → about 0.3 (light grey) within 2 frames;
  - then it darkens to full #222 over about 0.3 s;
  - while it slides about 20–40 px (at 720 w) into its slot, mostly from the right and sometimes from slightly above or below the baseline;
  - a mild blur may clear as it settles (not verified frame by frame).
  - The line looks loosely spaced while it builds, then tightens. Nothing ever bounces.
- **Caps/payoff words** use the same move but bigger and slower (0.4 s). **Tracking-in** (wide letter-spacing collapsing to normal) is saved for the CTA words ("HUMAN", "They Will Never Know AI Wrote It."). A **left-to-right box wipe** is used only once, in the hook.
- **Easing:** strong ease-out for everything that enters (cubic/expo-out feel, settling in 0.25–0.4 s); ease-in for exits (the group accelerates up or down and is cut mid-move). There is no overshoot.
- **Transitions (6 kinds, 0.15–0.35 s):**
  1. **slide-out + hard cut** (group slides up or down 30–100 px with an ease-in, then a cut): 3.4, 20.75, 32.6
  2. **blur-to-grey dissolve with a washed-out fade-in** (blur ramps up and the frame dims to a grey vignette, then the new scene fades in at low contrast and gains full contrast): 13.4 (via a white flash), 18.3, 27.1, 40.7
  3. **text-only cross-fade** inside a persistent layout: 15.7, 44.7
  4. **hard cut**: 4.85, 34.0, 47.1, 51.0, 55.45, 60.2
  5. **motion-blur whip** on the book: 52.5
  6. **content cross-fade inside a card** on the pillar: 36.2, 37.0
- **Objects enter from the frame edges, along a clear path:** the phone drops from the top, the pillar and logo rise from the bottom, the book slides from the right, the discs slide along their rails, the title line slides from the left. Then they hold almost still (very slow drift); the only continuous motion is the screen recording inside the card.
- **Scene changes sit on sentence boundaries,** and the 3 "First / Second / Third" headers each get a fresh layout. Within a sentence, nothing moves except the incoming words.

## Recreate-it notes (HyperFrames)
- 1080x1920, 30 fps (scale every px value above by 1.5). Background `#F6F6F6`, text `#222`, entering-word grey `#C8C8C8`. Add an optional centred dotted grid patch (`background-image` with 1 px dashed lines every 110 px at 1080 w, colour `#E4E4E4`, `mask-image: radial-gradient(...)`).
- **Word engine:** run whisper with word timestamps (as here) and render each word as a `<span>`. GSAP per word at `t = start − 0.1`: `fromTo({opacity:0, x:40, color:'#C8C8C8'}, {opacity:1, x:0, duration:0.15})` followed by `to({color:'#222', duration:0.3, ease:'power3.out'})`. Optionally add a random ±8 px `y` start for some words. Lines are pre-laid-out (fixed stack, centred) so words reveal into their final slots; the block never re-flows.
- Payoff word: same move at 1.4x size, bold/caps, 0.4 s. CTA word: `letterSpacing: '0.6em' → '0em'` plus the grey→black fade, 0.35 s.
- Hook label box: a black `div` with `clip-path: inset(0 100% 0 0)` → `inset(0 0 0 0)` over 0.45 s ease-out, white bold text inside, `box-shadow: 0 12px 24px rgba(0,0,0,.25)`.
- Scene changes: for the blur dissolve, animate the outgoing scene's `filter: blur(0→24px) brightness(1→0.85)` over 0.15 s, then fade the incoming scene in from `opacity:0.4; filter: contrast(0.5) brightness(1.3)` to normal over 0.3 s. For slide-out + cut, animate the outgoing group `y: 0 → −150` with `power2.in` over 0.25 s, then cut.
- Mock-ups: PNG or rendered HTML cards with `box-shadow: 0 30px 60px rgba(0,0,0,.18)`. Bring them in from the nearest frame edge with `power3.out`, 0.35–0.5 s, then let them drift 1–2% (scale or y) for the rest of the scene. A short muted `<video>` inside a rounded card gives the "live" feel.
- Keep a persistent element (the two toggle discs) across 2–3 consecutive text layouts to hold continuity while the words change.
